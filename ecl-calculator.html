IND AS 109 Para 5.5.15 — Simplified approach

An entity shall always measure the loss allowance at an amount equal to lifetime expected credit losses for trade receivables or contract assets that do not contain a significant financing component. This is mandatory — not an option.

IND AS 109 Para 5.5.17 — Provision matrix

An entity may use a practical expedient such as a provision matrix when measuring expected credit losses for trade receivables. A provision matrix specifies fixed provision rates depending on the number of days the trade receivable has been outstanding.

Forward-looking information (Para B5.5.51)

Historical default rates must be adjusted for forward-looking information — macroeconomic conditions, customer sector outlook, debtor-specific credit changes. Pure historical rates are not sufficient under IND AS 109 without a forward-looking overlay.

Disclosure — IND AS 107

Disclose: movement in the loss allowance account, maximum credit exposure, concentration of credit risk, and the basis for determining loss rates. The provision matrix itself should be disclosed as a key estimate in the significant accounting policies note.

Frequently asked questions — ECL & IND AS 109

Under IND AS 109, companies must recognise a loss allowance for expected credit losses on all financial assets (trade receivables, loans, contract assets, lease receivables). Unlike the old incurred-loss model under AS 26, ECL is forward-looking — you provision for losses expected to happen, not just those already incurred. For trade receivables without a significant financing component, the simplified approach using a provision matrix is mandatory.
Start with historical write-off data — for each ageing bucket, compute the % of receivables that were eventually written off over the past 3–5 years. Then adjust upward or downward for forward-looking factors: economic conditions, the specific debtor's industry outlook, customer credit ratings, and management's assessment of recoverability. Document the basis thoroughly — this is a key accounting estimate and auditors will scrutinise it.
Yes — significantly. Under AS 26 (IGAAP), provision was made only for losses that had already occurred (incurred-loss model). Under IND AS 109, you must provision for losses expected to occur even if there is no specific evidence of default yet. The provision matrix applies a loss rate to all receivables in each bucket — including current, not-yet-due receivables — not just those showing signs of impairment.
At minimum, at each reporting date (year-end and interim periods). The loss rates must be reviewed for changes in historical experience and updated for changes in forward-looking information. For example, if the economic outlook has deteriorated or a major customer sector is under stress, the rates should be revised upward even if historical data does not yet reflect it.
